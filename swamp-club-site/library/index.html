SOPHIE’S READING SHELF

The Library

books and papers worth a corner of your brain.

Sophie reads across: cognitive science, reinforcement learning, philosophy of science, 1950s economics, Toyota factory floors, modern AI papers. This is what’s on the shelf. Start with the LOOMUS Distill reading list (the 11 below) — each one underwrites one pillar of the Playbook.

I · Network Value

PILLAR I

Three competing laws of how networks scale. Metcalfe (1995) said N². Briscoe et al (2006) argued N log N. Reed (1999) went further: groups form, so 2^N. Each describes a different layer of the same phenomenon.

Metcalfe’s Law: A Network’s Value Grows as N²
Robert Metcalfe · 1995
the classic. short, foundational, often misquoted — everyone should read the actual claim before invoking it.
REFERENCE →
Metcalfe’s Law is Wrong
Briscoe, Odlyzko, Tilly · IEEE Spectrum 2006
a sharper corrective: V ∝ N log N. Worth reading specifically to argue with.
PDF →
That Sneaky Exponential—Beyond Metcalfe’s Law
David P. Reed · 1999
Reed’s Law: groups form within networks, so value scales 2^N. The deepest of the three.
ESSAY →

II · Bayesian Inference

PILLAR II

Knowing is not boolean. It is a distribution. Jaynes is the cult classic; once you read it, frequentist statistics never quite recovers.

Probability Theory: The Logic of Science
E. T. Jaynes · Cambridge UP · 2003 (posthumous)
the textbook for people who want to actually think Bayesian, not just calculate. dense, gorgeous, life-altering.
BOOK →
An Essay towards solving a Problem in the Doctrine of Chances
Thomas Bayes · 1763
the original. 21 pages. Read it for the historical chills.
PDF →

III · Multi-Armed Bandit

PILLAR III

The bible (Lattimore-Szepesvári, free) plus the original UCB paper. If you played Mushroom Bog — this is where UCB came from.

Bandit Algorithms
Lattimore & Szepesvári · Cambridge UP · 2020
the comprehensive modern reference. Free PDF. intimidating, then quietly addictive.
PDF →
Finite-Time Analysis of the Multiarmed Bandit Problem
Auer, Cesa-Bianchi, Fischer · 2002
the original UCB paper. You played this algorithm in the Mushroom Bog. read it after.
PDF →

IV · Reinforcement Learning & RLHF

PILLAR IV

Sutton & Barto for the foundation; Christiano for the leap to learning from human preferences; Ouyang for the production system that became ChatGPT.

Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction (2nd ed.)
Sutton & Barto · 2018
the textbook. Free online. if you read one book about how machines learn from feedback, this is it.
FREE →
Deep Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences
Christiano, Leike et al · NeurIPS 2017
the paper that made RLHF possible. teaching an agent from "I like this one better" instead of a hand-coded reward.
ARXIV →
Training Language Models to Follow Instructions (InstructGPT)
Ouyang et al · OpenAI · 2022
OpenAI’s blueprint for how a raw LM became actually useful. the engineering version of Christiano’s idea.
ARXIV →

V · Falsifiability

PILLAR V

Popper is 90 years old and still surgical. The line between knowledge and belief is whether a claim can be proven wrong — not whether it’s convincing.

The Logic of Scientific Discovery
Karl Popper · 1934
the founding text on what counts as knowledge vs belief. still painful to AI/ML researchers in the right way.
REFERENCE →

VI · Compounding

PILLAR VI

Two unlikely siblings: Solow’s 1956 econ paper that introduced compounding growth to mainstream economics, and Toyota’s kaizen — small steps, no stopping — expressed as a manufacturing philosophy.

A Contribution to the Theory of Economic Growth
Robert Solow · QJE · 1956
the paper that gave economics compounding growth. Solow won the Nobel for it. 38 pages, then time does the rest.
REFERENCE →
Toyota Production System: Beyond Large-Scale Production
Taiichi Ohno · 1988
where kaizen came from. tiny steps, no stopping. arguably the most important business book ever written.
REFERENCE →

VII · Constitutional AI

PILLAR VII

Anthropic’s line of work: give the model a constitution (principles), not just examples. ReST closes the loop — models train themselves on their own best outputs.

Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback
Bai, Kadavath et al · Anthropic · 2022
how Claude was shaped. principles before content. the manifesto.
ARXIV →
Reinforced Self-Training (ReST) for Language Modeling
Gulcehre et al · 2023
closing the loop: models train themselves on their own best outputs. the obvious next step.
ARXIV →
